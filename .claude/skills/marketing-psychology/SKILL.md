# Marketing Psychology & Mental Models

64 named psychological frameworks and mental models for understanding buyer behavior and designing more effective marketing.

## How to Use This Skill

Identify which stage of the funnel or which challenge you're facing, then pick the relevant models from the quick reference table. Apply models to diagnose, not just decorate — ask "does this principle explain why customers behave this way?"

Key questions before applying:
1. What specific behavior are you trying to influence?
2. What does your customer believe before encountering your marketing?
3. Where in the journey (awareness → consideration → decision) is this?
4. What's currently preventing the desired action?
5. Have you tested this with real customers?

## Foundational Thinking Models

1. **First Principles** — Break problems to basic truths; build solutions upward using repeated questioning
2. **Jobs to Be Done** — Customers "hire" products for outcomes, not features (drill = hole, not drill)
3. **Circle of Competence** — Stay within expertise areas; venture outside with learning or expert help
4. **Inversion** — Ask "What guarantees failure?" to identify what to avoid
5. **Occam's Razor** — Simplest explanation is usually correct; avoid overcomplicating
6. **Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)** — ~80% of results come from ~20% of efforts
7. **Local vs. Global Optima** — Local optimization may ignore larger systemic problems
8. **Theory of Constraints** — Every system has one bottleneck limiting throughput; fix that first
9. **Opportunity Cost** — Every choice forecloses alternatives; compare against next-best option
10. **Law of Diminishing Returns** — Additional investment yields progressively smaller gains
11. **Second-Order Thinking** — Consider effects of effects, not just immediate results

## Understanding Buyers & Human Psychology

1. **Fundamental Attribution Error** — We attribute others' behavior to character rather than circumstances
2. **Mere Exposure Effect** — Familiarity breeds liking; repeated exposure increases preference
3. **Availability Heuristic** — Likelihood judged by how easily examples come to mind
4. **Confirmation Bias** — Seeking information that confirms existing beliefs; ignoring contradictory evidence
5. **The Lindy Effect** — Longer something survives, longer it's likely to continue
6. **Mimetic Desire** — Desire is socially contagious; people want what others want
7. **Sunk Cost Fallacy** — Continuing investment based on past investment despite poor current returns
8. **Endowment Effect** — We value things more once we own them
9. **IKEA Effect** — We value things more when we've invested effort in creating them
10. **Zero-Price Effect** — "Free" triggers disproportionate preference vs. even very-low-cost alternatives
11. **Hyperbolic Discounting / Present Bias** — Strong preference for immediate vs. future rewards
12. **Status-Quo Bias** — Preference for current state; change feels inherently risky
13. **Default Effect** — Pre-selected options are powerfully accepted; defaults win
14. **Paradox of Choice** — Too many options overwhelm and paralyze decisions
15. **Goal-Gradient Effect** — Effort accelerates as goals approach; progress visualization motivates
16. **Peak-End Rule** — Experiences judged by peak moment and ending, not average
17. **Zeigarnik Effect** — Unfinished tasks occupy mind more than completed ones
18. **Pratfall Effect** — Competent people become more likable when they show small flaws
19. **Curse of Knowledge** — Experts struggle to explain simply what they know well
20. **Mental Accounting** — Money treated differently based on source or intended use
21. **Regret Aversion** — People avoid actions that might cause future regret
22. **Bandwagon Effect / Social Proof** — People follow what others do; popularity signals quality

## Influencing Behavior & Persuasion

1. **Reciprocity Principle** — People feel obligated to return favors; give value first
2. **Commitment & Consistency** — Once committed, people want to stay consistent with that commitment
3. **Authority Bias** — People defer to experts and authority figures
4. **Liking / Similarity Bias** — People say yes to those similar to themselves
5. **Unity Principle** — Shared identity drives influence; "one of us" is powerful
6. **Scarcity / Urgency Heuristic** — Limited availability increases perceived value
7. **Foot-in-the-Door** — Small request escalation; compliance compounds over time
8. **Door-in-the-Face** — Large request followed by retreat makes reasonable ask seem better
9. **Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory** — Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good
10. **Anchoring Effect** — First number seen heavily influences all subsequent judgments
11. **Decoy Effect** — Adding inferior third option makes one original option look better by comparison
12. **Framing Effect** — How something is presented changes perception even when facts are identical
13. **Contrast Effect** — Things seem different depending on comparison context

## Pricing Psychology

1. **Charm Pricing / Left-Digit Effect** — Prices ending in 9 feel significantly lower than round numbers
2. **Rounded-Price (Fluency) Effect** — Round numbers feel premium; easier to process cognitively
3. **Rule of 100** — Under $100: percentage discounts seem larger. Over $100: absolute discounts seem larger
4. **Price Relativity / Good-Better-Best** — Prices are judged relative to the options presented alongside them
5. **Mental Accounting (Pricing)** — Framing the same price differently changes perception ($1/day vs. $30/month)

## Design & Delivery Models

1. **Hick's Law** — Decision time increases with number and complexity of choices
2. **AIDA Funnel** — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action
3. **Rule of 7** — Prospects need ~7 touchpoints before converting
4. **Nudge Theory / Choice Architecture** — Small presentation changes significantly influence decisions without restricting options
5. **BJ Fogg Behavior Model** — Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt
6. **EAST Framework** — Make behaviors Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely
7. **COM-B Model** — Behavior requires Capability, Opportunity, and Motivation
8. **Activation Energy** — High initial energy required prevents action; reduce friction to lower it
9. **North Star Metric** — One metric capturing core delivered value; focus creates alignment
10. **The Cobra Effect** — Incentives backfiring, producing the opposite of intended results
11. **Probabilistic Thinking** — Estimating likelihoods; planning for multiple outcomes

## Growth & Scaling Models

1. **Feedback Loops** — Output becomes input; positive loops accelerate, negative loops decline
2. **Compounding** — Small consistent gains accumulate into large results; early gains matter most
3. **Network Effects** — Product becomes more valuable as more people use it
4. **Flywheel Effect** — Sustained effort creates self-maintaining momentum
5. **Switching Costs** — Cost of changing to competitors; high costs create natural retention
6. **Exploration vs. Exploitation** — Balancing trying new things with optimizing what works
7. **Critical Mass / Tipping Point** — Threshold where growth becomes self-sustaining
8. **Survivorship Bias** — Focusing on successes while ignoring invisible failures

## Additional Models

- **Map ≠ Territory** — Models and data represent reality but aren't reality itself
- **Barbell Strategy** — Combine extreme safety with small high-risk/high-reward bets
