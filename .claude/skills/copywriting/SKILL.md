# Copywriting

Write marketing copy that is clear, compelling, and drives action — for landing pages, homepages, pricing pages, feature pages, and ads.

## Before Writing

Check for product marketing context first. Then gather:

1. **Page Purpose** — type of page, primary action you want visitors to take
2. **Audience** — ideal customer, their problem, objections, language they use
3. **Product/Offer** — what's being sold, key differentiator, transformation, proof points
4. **Context** — traffic source, visitor awareness level

## Core Principles

**Clarity Over Cleverness** — Straightforward language wins. If a reader has to pause to decode it, you've lost them.

**Benefits Over Features** — Features describe the product. Benefits describe the outcome for the customer. Lead with outcomes.

**Specificity Over Vagueness** — "Reduce follow-up time by 60%" beats "save time." Avoid buzzwords like "streamline," "empower," "leverage."

**Customer Language Over Company Language** — Use the words your customers use to describe their problem, not your internal vocabulary.

**One Idea Per Section** — Each section earns one job. Don't cram multiple points into a single block.

## Writing Style Rules

1. Simple over complex
2. Specific over vague
3. Active over passive
4. Confident over qualified
5. Show over tell
6. Honest over sensational

**Quick quality check:**
- Jargon that would confuse an outsider?
- Sentences doing too much?
- Passive voice present?
- Unnecessary exclamation points?
- Buzzwords without substance?

## Best Practices

**Be Direct** — "Start your free trial" beats "You may want to consider getting started."

**Use Rhetorical Questions** — Sparingly. "Tired of chasing approvals?" — only when the answer is obviously yes.

**Use Analogies** — When a concept is unfamiliar, an analogy collapses the gap. One per page max.

**Humor** — Only when it fits the brand voice. Never at the expense of clarity.

## Headline Formulas

- `{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}`
- `The {category} for {audience}`
- `Never {unpleasant event} again`
- `{Question highlighting main pain point}`

## CTA Copy Guidelines

**Formula:** `[Action Verb] + [What They Get] + [Qualifier if needed]`

**Avoid:** Submit / Sign Up / Learn More / Click Here / Get Started

**Use:** Start Free Trial / Get [Specific Thing] / See [Product] in Action / Create Your First [Thing] / Download the Guide

Examples:
- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Get the Complete Checklist"
- "See Pricing for My Team"

## Page Structure

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Above the fold | Hook — headline + subhead + primary CTA |
| Social Proof | Build credibility early |
| Problem/Pain | Show you understand them |
| Solution/Benefits | Connect features to outcomes |
| How It Works | Reduce complexity |
| Objection Handling | FAQ, comparisons, guarantees |
| Final CTA | Recap value + action |

## Page-Specific Guidance

**Homepage** — Cast widest net. Lead with category + key differentiator. Support multiple ICPs in subheadings.

**Landing Page** — Single offer, single CTA. Remove navigation. Message-match the ad that brought them.

**Pricing Page** — Lead with value, not price. Use anchoring. Name plans after customer outcomes, not sizes.

**Feature Page** — Start with the job the feature does, not what the feature is.

**About Page** — Origin story + mission + team humanity. Not a press release.

## Voice and Tone

Adjust along two axes:
- **Formality:** casual ↔ formal
- **Personality:** playful ↔ serious, bold ↔ understated, technical ↔ accessible

Default: confident, direct, human. Never corporate.
